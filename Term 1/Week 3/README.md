**Project title:** Inclu

**My pair partner:** Iqbal

**Tool we had to use:** LLM Gemini

**SDG we had to address:** SDG 10 - Reduced Inequalities (SDG 10.2)

**What problem does it solve, and for whom?**

: Our platform "Inclu" solves a real problem for a specific user: a newly arrived immigrant in The Hague who feels isolated and does not know where to find support, activities, or communities that match their interests, background, and needs. Many people in this situation are overwhelmed by scattered information, language barriers, and uncertainty about which organisations are trustworthy or relevant for them.

Inclu helps this user by bringing together local community groups, social support organisations, and activity categories in one simple and welcoming experience. Instead of searching across many websites, they can select their interests, such as sports, culture, education, or welbeing, and quickly discover communitities that are likely to feel welcoing and useful. This reduces the time, stress, and confusion involved in finding a place to belong.

**What did you build?**

: We built Inclu, a Python-native Streamlit web app that helps newcomers and socially excluded people discover communities, activities, and support in The Hague. Users can explore categories, view community guides with joining steps and links, or complete a personal form to receive AI-powered recommendations tailored to their interests, language, age group, and location.

**Ethical reflection - what are the risks of your tool? Who could it harm?**
: Firstly, there is a risk of AI generating inaccurate or outdated community resources. AI models can hallucinate information, and community resources change frequently organisations move, close, or change their services. An AI-generated resource that does not exist could waste a user's time or erode trust.

Secondly, there is a risk of excluding people who lack digital literacy or internet access. A digital tool inherently requires internet access and basic digital literacy. Newcomers who are most isolated may be least likely to use an online tool, creating a paradox where the most vulnerable are excluded from the solution.

Thirdly, there is a risk of stereotyping migrants or socially isolated people. Framing the problem around "migrants" or "newcomers" risks reinforcing stereotypes that all migrants are lonely, socially excluded, or in need of help. The evidence shows that loneliness and participation gaps are statistical patterns, not universal characteristics. Many migrants have strong social networks and participate actively in community life indeed.
